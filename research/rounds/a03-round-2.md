## R4 — depth findings (Zollner ch. 7, Tier 1 monograph)

- §7.7.4.4 (p. 7-77): power absorption at a bearing is proportional to string wave impedance W = √(Tμ) and bearing conductance G; with G = 0.001 s/kg an E2 string loses 0.22–0.44 % per reflection. The small-loss reflection relation a² ≈ 4·W·G reproduces that range (spike `physics_oracle.py`, C-023).
- §7.5.3 (p. 7-29): measured conductance mostly below 0.01 s/kg (Fleischer 2006). C-025.
- §7.5.4 (p. 7-46/47) and §7.7.4.4 (p. 7-110): neck bending modes near 200 Hz and 450 Hz (Les Paul: 208 Hz, node at bridge and 10th fret; 445 Hz, nodes at bridge, 12th and 2nd fret); asymmetric headstocks split the first by up to ±50 Hz; low neck modes shift with how the guitar is supported. Below about 1 kHz neck resonances very selectively determine string damping; above it, bridge and string processes; for solid bodies, practically no influence of the wood in the treble. C-024, C-026, C-027.
- §7.8 (p. 7-111): the 'specialist journalism' collection reports contradictory statements on neck joints and fingerboards, including a measurement of equal decay times for glued-in and bolt-on necks (G&B 3/97). §7.1: the neck-body joint 'must not dissipate any significant amount of vibration energy'; neck-through removes the joint. The research document's bolt-on 'snap' and faster decay, and its fingerboard loss multipliers, have no measured support. C-028, C-029.
- §7.10.1 (p. 7-130) and §7.11 (p. 7-139..141): a bridge conductance of about 10⁻⁴ s/kg (solid body) has practically no effect on sustain; on semi-acoustic and hollow guitars with a free top the bridge conductance becomes a main damping component up to about 2 kHz. C-030.
